About

My path into neuroscience started in a brain-rehabilitation setting, working with people recovering from traumatic brain injury and neurodegenerative disease. That experience set the question I have followed ever since: how does the brain give rise to thought and behavior, and how can understanding it help people?

Each step in my career has zoomed out a level, from molecules, to systems, to the working human brain.

I began at the molecular level, engineering proteins to study Alzheimer's at Michigan State. At Stanford I moved up to whole systems, spending three years on neuroinflammation and neuroprotection research and contributing to work published in Science. That pull toward the bigger picture is what brought me to Dartmouth, where I now record directly from the human brain while people make real decisions.

Along the way I became as drawn to the computational side as the biology: designing the experiments, building the analysis pipelines, and finding the signals in the data. Today my focus is computational neuroscience, and on moving that work out of the lab toward tools that reach people.

What I work with

Neural data & electrophysiology

Human EEG and intracranial EEG (iEEG) recording, experimental and task design (PsychoPy), signal processing (MNE-Python), spike sorting (SpikeInterface).

Machine learning & statistics

Supervised and unsupervised machine learning (scikit-learn), regression modeling, time-frequency analysis, cluster-based permutation testing, FDR correction.

Programming & infrastructure

Python, R, MATLAB, SQL, Git, SLURM / HPC clusters, Singularity containers.

Selected work

iEEG analysis pipeline

End-to-end Python/MNE pipeline for human intracranial EEG: preprocessing, time-frequency feature extraction, and rigorous statistics. Part of thesis work showing that neural oscillatory activity tracks decision-making behavior.

Python · MNE · time-frequency analysis · statistics

Roulette decision-making task

A human risky-decision-making experiment built in PsychoPy to probe loss-chasing behavior, with photodiode-based neural synchronization for simultaneous intracranial recording.

PsychoPy · experimental design · human subjects

ML for gene-expression classification

Supervised machine-learning models classifying brain inflammatory gene-expression patterns in dementia from human RNA-seq data, with feature selection to identify discriminative markers. First-author IEEE publication.
